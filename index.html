MSc Research Dissertation — Length Generalisation in Transformer Sequence Models

View dissertation on GitHub
  • Designed and conducted 45 controlled, multi-seed experiments across three algorithmic tasks and five positional encoding methods, using PyTorch and AWS GPUs to evaluate Transformer generalisation under sequence-length distribution shifts up to 1,024 tokens.
  • Applied statistical evaluation across random seeds, attention statistics and position-wise error analysis to distinguish model underfitting, optimisation instability and out-of-distribution generalisation failures.
  • Developed a multi-seed baseline selection methodology to control for random initialisation effects, ensuring model comparisons were based on stable configurations rather than isolated experimental outcomes.
  • Investigated mixed-length training, curriculum learning and randomised padding, using carry-condition and dependency-distance diagnostics to analyse how sequence structure affected model performance and extrapolation.
  • Produced reproducible experimental results and a failure taxonomy separating training performance, seed-dependent variability and systematic generalisation errors, enabling more rigorous interpretation beyond aggregate accuracy.
